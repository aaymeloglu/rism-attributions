RISM: naming the anonymous

RISM catalogues about 1.6 million music manuscripts and prints; about 297,000 of them are listed under “Anonymus”. Most of those carry incipits, the opening bars of movements or sections in Plaine & Easie code. This project searches every anonymous incipit against the attributed ones, keeps matches that no other composer shares, and compares every candidate incipit by incipit. So far 105 anonymous copies are the same music as an attributed copy (19 on two or more differently numbered incipits, 86 on a single one). Of those, 81 are not noted in RISM and carry a single, unqualified attribution; 8 more are new but their composer is disputed, uncertain, a bare surname or known only from a modern copy; 16 turned out to be noted in RISM already. 32 more are probably the same music (transposed, arranged, or only partly encoded) and 9 are unresolved. The automatic score compares pitch contour only, ignoring rhythm; it finds candidates, and the rendered incipits on each page are the evidence. A match transfers RISM’s attribution of the other copy, so it cannot settle a disputed authorship, and composers’ printed thematic catalogues may already list some copies. Research and incipit comparison by Claude (an LLM); an independent review by Codex (another LLM) led to the current labels. Method in METHOD.md.
